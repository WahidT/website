        05.1Where the models act

        A model trained on physical behaviour acts on a thing with mass: a beam of X-rays, a boom of spray nozzles, a bank of inverters.

        05.2The barrier is the record

        In an open market a model compresses the technical lead. It cannot compress the evidence behind an approved product, because clinical validation, regulatory history and real-world performance accumulate only over time.

        05.3A rising bar reverses the advantage

        Existing evidence supports the requirement it was collected for, so a company designed for the new requirement is already gathering the data that one demands. hmm looks for that transition: the point at which regulation changes what must be proven and a younger company is already proving it.

        05.4Cheaper to reach the bar, and the bar still sorts

        Reaching a regulatory milestone costs less than it did, from discovery through to submission, so more companies now reach the bar than reached it before.

        Permission does two different jobs.

        A necessity system runs to as many as ten stages. The gate marks the stage whose value is hardest to compete away, which is why hmm's capital points there.

        An approval is issued against a named site and the process running on it. Moving assembly to a building outside the certificate restarts three filings: the new site registers with the regulator as a manufacturing location, the conformity investigation, in which the regulator examines the process on that site against the standard, runs again there, and the change clears before the approval reads on the new line. Those three filings are the qualification clock, and an acquirer buys the approval because buying it keeps the clock where it stands. An entry changes hands administratively, and the clinical validation, the regulatory history and the real-world performance behind it stay where they were built, because a regulator grants an entry and time accumulates a record.

        The data that locates a company also shows what its approval will cost, in time and in evidence, which is why every deal is read four ways against the instrument that gates it: which instrument it must clear, what evidence that demands, how far the field has already moved, and where the company sits on that path today. An AI diagnostic clears Japan's conditional early-approval route, or Australia's software-as-a-medical-device rule, on clinical evidence. Annual United States authorisations of AI-enabled devices rose from a maximum of six a year before 2016 to 331 in 2025, on the counts Singh and others published in 2025 and Golshani and Joseph in 2026.

        Companies whose product cannot be sold without an approval reach a liquidity event at 17.73%, against 6.08% where no approval gates the sale, measured on 12,664 companies across the three markets at an odds ratio of 3.33. The tier between them, where a buyer sits under a compliance obligation, reaches 5.82% and sits with the unregulated rate.

        Three markets, one distributed experiment.

        Each market leads a different one of the three systems, so the union covers all three with no overlap, and that is the reason to hold all three. Route follows the market: Australia and New Zealand realise through trade sale, and Japan is the only one of the three where listings lead.

        Australia

        Deepest of the three on health. A company selling a device or a diagnostic here holds an entry on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods, and supply without an entry is unlawful. A second gate follows the first, and Australian runway is spent at it: registration reaches a Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme listing in 466 days on Medicines Australia's measure of new molecular entities registered 2016 to 2021, against the 384-day average it reports across twenty OECD countries. That series is measured on medicines, and no device-specific series resolves, so a diagnostic runs its own second gate through the Medical Services Advisory Committee to an item number.

        Japan

        Japan's second gate is the fastest of the three, so a medicine that clears reaches a reimbursement listing far ahead of the Australian calendar on that same medicines series. The agency accepts no application direct from a foreign manufacturer, so a designated marketing authorisation holder is a precondition of filing and the technical file is submitted in Japanese. The research channels are enumerable and mostly untranslated, among them 29 approved technology licensing offices as at 1 April 2026.

        New Zealand

        It operates no pre-market approval regime for medical devices. A sponsor notifies a database within 30 working days of becoming the sponsor, and the regulations prohibit presenting that listing as an endorsement of the device's safety or suitability. So a New Zealand device company earns its record offshore at a foreign regulator and buys speed to first deployment at home. That window has an end date waiting to be set. The Therapeutic Products Act 2023 was struck out on 18 December 2024 before its main provisions commenced, and no replacement Bill had been introduced as at May 2026. A company deployed under the notification regime holds the record a replacement would ask for, so the change sorts in its favour. Agriculture runs the other way: Halter's P5 collar was approved in Victoria in February 2026 and in South Australia in July 2026, each a state approval under amended animal welfare regulations.
