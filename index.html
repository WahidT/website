        The mandate

        hmm backs seed companies in the three systems a population cannot do without: how it is powered, how it is fed, how it is healed. What changes most over the next two decades sits inside those three. Demand for them holds through every cycle, but only with a regulator's approval.

        Regulators who aren't ready for the next wave of innovation brought by physical AI and World Models.

        05 · AI · the accelerating layer

        AI can copy the model quickly. The evidence behind an approval has to be earned again.

        05.1The barrier is the record

        In an open market, AI compresses the technical lead. It cannot copy the evidence behind an approved product: clinical validation, regulatory history and real-world performance accumulated over time. A newcomer may match the leader's scan-reading accuracy and still lack the record on which regulators, clinicians and buyers rely. The model can be rebuilt. The evidence must be earned again.

        05.2A rising bar can reverse the advantage

        The record usually protects the incumbent. That can change when the standard changes. Existing evidence may support the previous requirement, while a company designed for the new one is already collecting the data it demands. hmm looks for that transition: the point at which regulation changes what must be proven and a younger company is already proving it.

        05.3AI widens the field; the gate still decides

        AI is lowering the cost of reaching a regulatory milestone, from discovery through to submission, so more companies can now reach the bar than could before. AI increases the number of companies capable of reaching the regulatory milestone; regulation determines which of them can commercialise. For hmm that widens the field of candidates and leaves the gate as the filter that sorts them.

        06 · Regulation · the decisive lens

        Regulation does two different jobs.

        06.AFor the fund · a locator

        A necessity system runs to as many as ten stages. The gate marks the stage whose value is hardest to compete away, and points hmm's capital there.

        hmm-REG-06.A
        06.BFor the company · the barrier

        The same gate is what a rival cannot cheaply copy, and what an acquirer pays a premium for. It also delays the company clearing it, and can shelter an incumbent as readily as a challenger.

        hmm-REG-06.B
        06.CFor the underwriter · the data

        The data that locates a company also shows what its approval will cost, in time and in evidence. Every deal is read four ways against the instrument that gates it: which instrument it must clear, what evidence that demands, how far the field has already moved, and where the company sits on that path today. An AI diagnostic clears Japan's PMDA conditional-approval pathway, or Australia's software-as-a-medical-device rule, on clinical evidence. The direction of travel is visible in the United States, where annual FDA authorisations of AI-enabled devices rose from a maximum of six a year before 2016 to 331 in 2025, the most in that agency's history. hmm underwrites a company by where it sits on that path.

        07 · Markets that live a few years ahead

        Three markets, one distributed experiment.

        Small markets, top-of-peer capital efficiency. New Zealand converts venture dollars into enterprise value at the highest rate in its OECD set; Australia produces unicorns per dollar at nearly twice the US rate. Their exit routes differ by market: Australia and New Zealand through trade sale, Japan through a live public market.

        08 · How we find them

        If the necessities are where the next wave of innovation sits, how do we find these founders?

        A

        Source before the first round

        These companies do not reliably enter through conventional venture networks. Many founders are still focused on the technical problem when hmm first approaches them. The fund maps a market, identifies relevant companies and contacts the teams before a formal raise begins. The GP used this outbound method in venture and later as an operator at a platform built to identify companies before they entered a sales pipeline.

        B

        Build the commercial side

        Technical capability is often already present. The unresolved questions are commercial: who buys, what budget pays, how large the reachable market is and how the company reaches its first customers. hmm works through those questions with the founders. The GP has spent a decade building and leading revenue functions for software and data companies selling across international markets.

        C

        Enter locally, scale internationally

        Most comparable exits in the market record hmm compiled are sales to overseas acquirers, while limited local capital continues to shape entry pricing. hmm invests before international capital becomes the price setter and helps founders build for the markets where their customers and eventual acquirers operate. The GP has worked on both sides of that expansion, leading commercial growth across APAC and into North America and Europe.

        They form in markets the global funds treat as insular and thin on exits. The record shows the innovation was there, mostly found or backed late.
