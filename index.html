Necessity is the thesis

Energy, food and care reach us through systems built for another technological era. Their physical foundations are now being rebuilt. In the power plant, the paddock and the clinic, founders are applying advances in intelligence, biology and engineering inside markets whose rules predate those capabilities.

These are systems society cannot afford to fail, so technology enters them by earning permission. We believe value in a necessity moves towards whoever holds its regulated layer, and we invest where that movement is already underway.

Three systems qualify on two conditions: demand that holds through every cycle, and failure counted in blackouts, shortages and untreated disease. We back the companies that need permission to sell into them, not the software that checks compliance.

        The gate

        The second build is the one that lasts.

        The first build proves the technology works. The second earns the right to sell it: the evidence, the qualified site, the quality system and the regulatory record.

        An approval is issued against a named site and the process running on it, so a competitor cannot copy the second build by copying the first. An acquirer who rebuilds the manufacturing restarts the qualification clock, which is why we believe buying the company usually costs less than replicating it.

        We believe artificial intelligence shortens the first build and leaves the second intact. A model compresses a technical lead. It cannot compress clinical validation, regulatory history or real-world performance, which accumulate only with time.

        How we find them

        We meet founders before the round exists.

        A

        Source before the first round

        These companies reach conventional venture networks late, if at all, and many founders are still working on the technical problem when we first meet them. We map each market, identify the companies inside it and approach the teams before a raise begins.

        B

        Work on the second build

        The technology usually works. The open questions are commercial: who buys, which budget pays, how large the reachable market is and how the company wins its first customers. We work through them with the founders.

        C

        Enter locally, scale internationally

        Exit routes differ by market, and thin local capital shapes entry pricing. We invest before international capital sets the price, then help founders build towards the markets where their customers and eventual acquirers operate.
