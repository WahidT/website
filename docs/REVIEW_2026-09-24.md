# hmm.ventures, a review of the whole site

Version 1.1, written 2026-09-24 on branch `claude/hmm-ventures-simplify-2gdh5h`, against `main` at
`9ad0b6e` (website #24 merged 2026-09-17). This is a review and a proposal, not a build. It is a
working document and not a distribution surface, so the prose gate refuses it by design.

**Ruled on this branch, 2026-09-24.** The general partner ruled the markets section out: "we can
probably remove this entire section". Section 7, "Three markets, one distributed experiment",
its table, `markets.js`, the specialisation snapshot `data/necessity_matrix.js` and its builder,
and the two guard blocks that reconciled them leave on this branch. R-D37 (2026-09-15), which
folded the market section into that table, is superseded by this ruling. The register snapshot
`data/market_strength.js` stays, because the screen's second filter and the register figure read
it. The counts sentence in the sourcing block ("The map holds 79 companies in Australia...") was
ruled out the same day, with its snapshot `data/catalogue_counts.js`; the guard that keeps every
catalogued name off the served pages stands. The rest of the proposal is unchanged and still
awaits the rulings in part 6.

**The brief.** Review the site as a whole and say how it can be simpler and more direct about the
thesis and the point of view, holding to two constraints the general partner set: it is a thesis
site rather than a fund pitch, and a reader must still be clear that hmm is a fund.

**Method.** The served text of every page was dumped by stripping markup (`index.html`, `bio.html`,
`for-llms.html`, `sources.html`, `instruments.html`, `404.html`), the script-rendered copy was read
at source (`sections.js`, `screen.js`, `markets.js`, the three `*_steps.js` files), and each finding
was checked against `brain/LANGUAGE.md` sections 7.0 to 7.3 and against `docs/STRUCTURE_2026-09-14.md`
so nothing proposed re-opens a ruling that has already closed. No browser run was made, so layout,
motion, weight and mobile are out of scope; `site-qa` covers those.

---

## Executive summary

The site's argument is right and its evidence is strong. What is wrong is where things sit. The
one-paragraph statement of the thesis exists on the site, word for word, and it sits on the page
written for language models rather than the page written for people. The homepage delivers the
same thesis across two sections and roughly six hundred words of mechanism, under headings that
describe a scene rather than assert a position. A reader who skims the headings, which is how a
page read in seconds is read, meets ten labels and none of them says what hmm believes or what hmm
is.

On the second constraint, the fund is named once, in the last sentence of the opener, and then
disappears until the sourcing block near the foot of the page. The three markets are first named
in the seventh section. The word "fund" appears in the body once. The title tag reads "regulated
seed venture", which is a category and not a noun a reader has met.

The proposal is four moves, in order of effect:

1. **Put the thesis and the identity on the first screen after the opener**, lifting the text
   that already exists on `for-llms.html`. Two short blocks, about sixty words, under the H1.
2. **Merge sections 05 and 06 into one section headed by the thesis sentence**, "Regulation
   locates the company, then protects it", with the tier-liquidity finding as the evidence and one
   paragraph each for the barrier and the why-now. About 570 words become about 230.
3. **Cut what the three system sections say twice**, keep the drawing and the unit paragraph, and
   decide whether the ten-stage schematic belongs on a public page at all.
4. **Rename the rail and the section headings so a first reader can follow them**, and trim
   `for-llms.html` to the thesis, dropping the four answers that sell the fund.

Each move is set out below with the observation, why it matters, the proposal and the ruling it
needs. Draft copy for the three new or rewritten blocks is in part 5, and every sentence in it is
either lifted from a page that already ships or marked as new.

## 2. Findings, ranked by effect

Each finding is an observation, then the judgement, then the proposal. Observations are checked
against the files named. Judgements are this review's and are marked as such.

### F1. The thesis is stated in full only on the language-model page

**Observed.** `for-llms.html` carries this under "The thesis":

> Necessity is the thesis. Two forces move together: what regulation newly requires, and what
> technology newly makes possible. As they advance, value migrates one way, towards whoever holds
> the regulated layer of a necessity. hmm invests where that migration is already underway.

And under "How to describe the mechanism accurately":

> Regulation locates the company, then protects it.

Neither sentence appears on `index.html`. The closest the homepage comes is mid-paragraph in
section 6: "The gate marks the stage whose value is hardest to compete away, which is why hmm's
capital points there."

**Judgement.** The page built for a machine to summarise carries the summary, and the page built
for a person makes the person assemble it. That is the inversion the brief is pointing at. The
`for-llms` text already clears the house rules, since it shipped, so lifting it costs nothing in
canon.

**Proposal.** The thesis paragraph moves to the homepage directly under the H1, in "we" form.
Draft in part 5, block B.

### F2. After the opener, nothing says what hmm is until the sourcing block

**Observed.** The opener's last sentence, "We lead seed rounds for founders rebuilding how
populations are powered, fed and healed", is the one identity statement on the first screen, and
it is correct under 7.3.4. It names the stage. It does not name the systems by their system names,
the markets, or the word "fund". The next statement of what hmm is comes in section 9, "the fund
maps a market". The `<title>` reads "regulated seed venture" and the meta description reads "a
seed fund backing the regulated technology behind the energy, food and healthcare systems across
Australia, Japan and New Zealand", which is the line the page body never says.

**Judgement.** The meta description is the sentence the brief asks for, and the site serves it to
crawlers and withholds it from readers. One line under the H1 settles "we are a fund" without
turning the page into a pitch, because it carries no figure, no term and no ask.

**The ruling this needs.** 7.3.4 puts the fund last and once, and 7.3.5 bans a market name, but
both govern the broadcast opener, defined in 7.3 as "the first forty to eighty words". The opener
is the ninety-five words the general partner set and it is untouched. The identity line sits after
the H1, which is after the opener, so on the section's own scope it is outside the rule. That is
this review's reading and the general partner should confirm it, because a stricter reading would
say the fund appears twice on the first screen.

**Proposal.** One line under the H1, before the thesis paragraph. Draft in part 5, block A. The
`<title>` changes to "hmm ventures · Necessity is the thesis · a seed fund".

### F3. Sections 05 and 06 carry the argument under headings that do not state it

**Observed.** Section 5's H2 is a thirty-word description of three machines at work, which is the
opener's second paragraph restated ("founders are applying advances in intelligence, biology and
engineering within markets governed by rules that predate those capabilities"). Its four sub-blocks
are numbered 05.1 to 05.4 with document-style labels. Section 6's H2, "Permission does two
different jobs", promises two jobs and the body never names them; the two jobs are the locator and
the barrier (LANGUAGE.md 7.3.2), and `for-llms.html` names them in five words. The tier-liquidity
finding, which is the strongest evidence on the site, sits as the fourth paragraph of section 6
with no heading over it. The qualification clock runs 110 words of filing procedure to reach one
conclusion, that an acquirer buys the approval to keep the clock where it stands.

**Judgement.** The two sections are one argument: regulation locates the value, the approval
protects it, the evidence is the exit rate, and the reason to act now is that capability has
arrived and the evidence record has not moved. Splitting it across two sections with a scene for a
heading is what makes a reader work for a conclusion the site already holds. The 05.x numbering
reads as a memorandum's table of contents on a page that is meant to be read in seconds.

**Proposal.** One section, headed "Regulation locates the company, then protects it." Four
paragraphs in this order: the locator (existing sentence), the evidence (the guarded tier-liquidity
sentence, verbatim), the barrier (the qualification clock in one sentence, with the three filings
kept because they are the mechanism), the why-now (05.2, 05.4 and 05.3 as one paragraph). The FDA
sentence and the register timeline stay as the figure. The four-way read paragraph ("every deal is
read four ways") is how the fund works rather than what it believes, and it moves to the sourcing
block or goes. Draft in part 5, block C. Estimated 570 words to 230.

**Guard.** The tier-liquidity sentence is parsed by `check-register-figures.mjs` against
`data/canon_tier_liquidity.json`. It moves as a byte-exact block or the build fails, which is the
intended behaviour.

### F4. The three system sections say each system three times before they say anything new

**Observed.** For Power, the card reads "The energy system: fuel and critical minerals, generation,
storage, and the grid to end use." The section's definition reads "Power is the energy system: a
resource extracted, refined, generated, carried across the grid, used, and its materials
recovered." The system intro reads "One chain, resource to end use and back to recovery." Eat and
Heal follow the same pattern. Only the unit paragraph ("The instance is a power plant, and it earns
per megawatt hour delivered...") and the one sentence on where the capital points ("hmm works one
layer down, on the storage and the critical minerals") add to the card. Below that, each system
runs a schematic of eight to ten stages, each stage opening a panel with an AI facet and a
regulation facet, which is 620 to 960 words of data per system.

**Judgement.** The card is the summary and the section is the depth, so the section should begin
where the card stopped. The ten-stage chain is the fund model's stage chain (MODEL_DOSSIER), an
internal instrument for locating a company inside a system. On a public page it is the most
detailed thing on the site and the least connected to the point of view, and it answers a question
a first reader has not asked. The drawings are the core identity and stay; the question is whether
the stage panels belong on this page or in the research conversation.

**Proposal.** Per system: the drawing, one sentence on where the capital points, the unit paragraph.
The definition and the system intro go, because the card carries them. The stage schematic stays
as the drawing it is, and the per-stage text panels are the general partner's decision: this
review recommends they leave the public page, on the 7.2 clearance test, because a stage chain
would not be worth publishing in a world where the fund did not exist, whereas the unit paragraph
would.

### F5. The rail and the headings use the estate's names, not the reader's

**Observed.** The rail reads Top, The fit, Power, Eat, Heal, Capability, Permission, The screen,
Markets, Sourcing, The conversation. "The fit" and "The screen" are names from the structure file
and the memorandum; a first reader meets them with no referent. Section 8's H2 is the second
filter's read line rather than the section's question, and the DOM carries section 8 before
section 7.

**Judgement.** The register opens a section on the question it answers (7.0). "Why Australia,
Japan and New Zealand" is the question the screen answers, and the elimination bar is the answer.
The rail is the page's table of contents and it should read as one.

**Proposal.**

| Now | Proposed | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The fit | The thesis | It is what the section is |
| Capability + Permission | The gate | One section after F3 |
| The screen | Why these markets | The question it answers |
| Sourcing | How we find them | The heading the section already carries in spirit |
| The conversation | The first conversation | Matches the copy |

Section 8's H2 becomes "Why Australia, Japan and New Zealand." The R-D26 opener check runs over
every heading, so each new heading is tested at build.

### F6. The page never says why these three systems

**Observed.** The absence test that ranks Power, Eat and Heal lives on the bio page ("it ranks
systems by what happens when they are absent") and in the `for-llms` answer to "Is necessity
circular?": "Two conditions decide it: demand that holds through a cycle, and a system whose
failure is counted in blackouts, shortages and untreated disease." The homepage names the three
and moves on.

**Judgement.** "Why these three" is the first objection a thesis reader raises, and the answer
exists in house form. One sentence above the cards closes it.

**Proposal.** The two-condition sentence, lifted from `for-llms.html`, placed between the thesis
paragraph and the cards. Draft in part 5, block B.

### F7. `for-llms.html` is longer than the homepage and half of it sells the fund

**Observed.** 1,634 words against the homepage's 1,007 static. The first half is the thesis, the
scope and the mechanism, and it is the best-written statement of the point of view on the site.
The second half is fourteen questions, of which four are fund questions rather than thesis
questions: "First-time fund, no track record", "Who prices the regulatory approval risk", "If this
is real, why have global funds not already done it", and "What are you deliberately not telling
me", whose answer names "the return model and portfolio construction" and "qualified investors".
The downside answer names "a reserve to carry a graduate", which is portfolio construction. The
page also quotes the site: "As the site puts it: AI can copy the model quickly, and the evidence
behind an approval has to be earned again." That sentence no longer appears on any page.

**Judgement.** Under 7.2 the public surface advocates a thesis and the private surface sells a
fund. The four fund answers fail the clearance test, and they are the answers a summariser will
surface first because they are the most quotable. The self-quotation is a page citing text it
retired.

**Proposal.** Keep "What hmm is", "The thesis", the three systems, the mechanism, the scope and
the primary sources. Keep the thesis questions: the exit-rate question, the circularity question,
the AI-erosion question, the denominator question, the listings question, the in-and-out question,
the impact question and the trust question. Drop the four fund answers and the reserve sentence.
Replace the self-quotation with the sentence the homepage will carry after F3. Estimated 1,634
words to about 900.

### F8. `instruments.html` is a public URL carrying review-harness text

**Observed.** The page is unlinked from every other page and serves lines such as "Cropped icon ·
seed 23 · accent --hmm-nec-power". The structure file records its placement as unsettled.

**Proposal.** Either the three instrument drawings get a place on the homepage or the bio page
with their captions, or the file leaves the published root and lives under `scripts/` with the
review harness it belongs to. Leaving a debug surface on a public domain is the one thing it should
not keep doing.

### F9. Voice: "hmm" in the third person against "we" at both ends

**Observed.** "We lead", "we ask" at the opener and the close; "hmm works", "hmm backs", "hmm's
read is", "hmm looks for", "hmm's capital points", "the fund maps", "hmm works through", "hmm
invests" in between. The bio page correctly runs "we" for the fund and "he" for the general partner
(GP 2026-09-03, recorded in the structure file).

**Proposal.** "We" throughout the homepage. Nine edits, none of which touches a guarded figure.

### F10. Two checks, not findings

- `sources.html` still lists the Safeguard Mechanism (Crediting) Amendment Act 2023 under
  Australia. The structure file's part 6 records that row leaving `data/reg_instruments.js` under
  R-D29 and says nothing about the sources page. Whether R-D29 intended the sources row to stay is
  a question for the ruling's owner, not this review.
- The GP page and the sources page are reachable only from the footer. A single-scroll page with a
  rail is the design, and this review does not propose a top navigation, but a reader who wants to
  know who wrote the page has to reach the bottom of it to find out. If a reviewer raises this, the
  answer is a two-item line at the top right rather than a menu.

### Block C. The gate, replacing sections 05 and 06

> **Regulation locates the company, then protects it.**
>
> A necessity system runs to as many as ten stages, and the gate marks the stage whose value is
> hardest to compete away. That is where our capital points.
>
> Companies whose product cannot be sold without an approval reach a liquidity event at 17.73%,
> against 6.08% where no approval gates the sale, measured on 12,664 companies across the three

>

>

> product, because clinical validation, regulatory history and real-world performance accumulate

Sources, paragraph by paragraph: the heading is lifted from `for-llms.html`. Paragraph one is

## 6. What would need to be true, and the rulings owed

1. **7.3.4 and 7.3.5 scope to the opener's forty to eighty words and not to the first screen.**
   If the general partner reads them as governing the whole first screen, block A cannot ship and
   the identity carries in the `<title>` and the rail only. This is the one ruling that changes
   the shape of the proposal.
2. **The per-stage panels are the fund's instrument rather than the site's argument.** If the
   general partner holds that the stage chain is part of the point of view, F4 keeps the panels
   and the page stays long by that amount. The drawings stay either way.
3. **The four `for-llms` fund answers are pitch rather than thesis.** If the general partner wants
   a summariser to be able to answer "first-time fund, why back it", the first-time-fund answer
   stays and the other three go.
4. **The rewritten paragraph three of block C is acceptable as new prose.** Otherwise the original
   moves whole.
5. **Every new heading clears R-D26 at build.** `check:openers` runs in the Netlify build and will
   refuse a regulator as actor or a pointer close. Block C's heading names regulation as the
   subject of "locates" and "protects"; 7.3.2 bans the regulator as a character, and a rule that
   locates is a relation rather than an actor, but it is the closest call in the draft and the
   check decides it.
